01How to use this

Each entry gives an id, a brief, and the constraints that make it fit. Feed a single entry to an image or model generator; do not feed the whole document at once, because the constraints are per-asset and a generator will average them.

A workable prompt is the brief, plus the art direction in §03, plus the faction livery in §04 if the asset belongs to one. The constraints are for whoever wires it in, not for the generator.

Priority

P0The game is visibly incomplete without it. Ships, stations, the HUD.
P1The game reads as unfinished without it. Effects, icons, core audio.
P2Polish. Music, ambience, faction logos, decals.

The one convention that will break everything

−Z is forward, +Y is up, +X is right. This is what shared/src/sim.rs steers with. A model authored nose-along-+Z flies backwards. The current placeholder rotates a cone −90° about X to point it down −Z; a properly authored asset should need no rotation at all.

Origin at the centre of mass — for a ship, roughly the centre of the hull, not the nose. The ship rotates about its origin, so an off-centre origin makes the hull swing rather than turn.

Scale

Ships are sized by hit_radius, the radius of the sphere weapons test against. A model must fit inside its hit sphere — anything outside it can be shot through.

ShipClass.visual_scale exists in the data model as a multiplier and is currently unused — ships render as primitive assemblies about 5 m long. Author to the table above and drop it. A model whose true size is a data-file multiplier is a model nobody can inspect.

Naming

<category>/<id>.<ext>, using the id from the data files verbatim: ships/kestrel.glb, icons/commodity/iron_ore.png, audio/weapon/pulse_1_fire.ogg. The ids are the join between this document and the code; renaming one breaks that join silently.

03Art direction

The reference is Freelancer (2003), not Elite Dangerous.

Hulls are readable at a glance and slightly stylised: clear silhouettes, flat panelled surfaces, visible engine glow, colour that says who built the thing. Not gritty photorealism, not cartoon. A player should be able to name a ship class from its outline at 2 km.

Space is dark and coloured. The clear colour is very nearly black with a blue cast. The key light is warm white at a low angle; ambient is cool and dim. That contrast is deliberate — it makes hulls read as lit objects in a dark place rather than as flat sprites.

The one rule

Emissive is information. Anything that glows in this game means something: engines are thrust, tracers are damage in flight, station lights are a place you can dock. Do not add decorative glow to hull panels — it makes the things that matter harder to see.

04Faction identity

Four factions. Each needs a livery, a logo that reads at 32 px, and a structure palette.

reach_compact The Reach Compact P2

The established power: industrial, bureaucratic, prosperous. White and navy over brushed steel; clean lines, everything stencilled with registration numbers. Logo: a stylised five-pointed star inside a ring, #1F3A6E on white. Their station is Halden — big, lit, obviously expensive to run.

wardens Compact Wardens P2

The Compact's enforcement arm, and a separate faction. Same hull shapes, different paint: white with a broad #F2B705 amber stripe along the flank and flashing amber running lights. Logo: the Compact star behind a shield. It must read at a glance as authority — a pilot should know from the silhouette and the stripe that shooting this is a decision.

traders_guild Traders Guild P2

Merchants, not soldiers. Worn olive and cream, heavy container hardpoints, mismatched replacement panels — working ships repaired badly for twenty years. Logo: a pair of scales over a cargo pod, #7A6A3F.

cinder_clans The Cinder Clans P2

Pirates, and the owners of Kessler Yard. Everything scavenged: exposed plating, asymmetric welded-on armour, no two panels the same shade of rust and charcoal. Deep red running lights instead of white. Logo: a broken ring, hand-painted rather than stencilled, #8C2020. Their outpost should look like a station somebody else built and they took.

05Ships

Three hulls, each flown by players and NPCs alike — an NPC is a ShipInput producer, not a different kind of object.

Hardpoint offsets are exact, in metres, in hull-local space, from assets/data/ships.ron. They are where muzzle flashes, mining beams and engine effects attach. Model the corresponding hardware at those points or the effects will float. The offsets are given at the placeholder's ~5 m scale: multiply by roughly four when authoring at full size, and keep the arrangement.

Jump gates P0

Two, one per system, each 500 m radius — a ring you fly into, not a door. Harrow Reach's is at (−5200, 300, −1400); Kessler Gap's at (0, 0, 2600).

A massive open ring with four or six anchor pylons, inactive by default, the interior holding a faint distortion that flares when a ship jumps. It should be visible from several kilometres — this is a navigation landmark, and a gate a player cannot find is a system they cannot leave. Include a slowly rotating outer collar so it reads as machinery rather than architecture.

Trade lane rings P1

Three lanes: Halden to the Kessler Gap gate and Halden to Harrow in Harrow Reach, the gate to Kessler Yard in Kessler Gap. Each has an end ring at both ends (enter within 300 m; currently a 120 m torus) and a smaller line ring every kilometre between, all facing along the lane. Currently amber #F2B34D emissive tori.

An end ring is a docking-ring-sized hoop on a short pylon, with a beacon that reads at several kilometres: it is where a pilot lines up. Line rings are lighter versions of the same shape and, in a row, should read as a route at a glance. Rings flashing in sequence as a ship rides past are optional, but that is what tells a pilot the lane is carrying them.

07Weapons and projectiles

Every weapon here is a projectile. There is no hitscan, and leading a target is a skill.

The projectile is a real object the player watches travel, so how it looks matters more than usual. Each weapon needs a muzzle model at its hardpoint, a projectile look, and audio (§11).

Impact effects P1

  • Energy — a bright flash and a brief circular scorch.
  • Kinetic — sparks and a puff of debris; visible ablation.
  • Explosive — a small fireball with a shockwave ring.
  • EMP — a blue-white electrical crawl across the target hull.

Shield impact must be distinct from a hull hit: shields absorb before hull, and a player needs to know which is being eaten. A hexagonal ripple spreading from the impact point across a spherical shield surface, brightest at the centre, fading in about 0.4 s.

07aEquipment

Seventeen modules, three of them switched on in flight, bolted into mounts by kind and size. Most are internal — a shield capacitor is a box in a bay — so the need here is icons first, hull attachments second.

Every module's effect is a percentage of the hull's own numbers, and the trade-offs are authored in pairs: a capacitor holds more and refills slower, an emitter array does the reverse. An icon set that reads those pairs as opposites is worth more than seventeen unrelated pictures.

Hull attachments P2

Five of the fourteen are plausibly visible from outside, and a ship that looks fitted is most of what makes fitting satisfying: the two plating modules as bolted plates sitting proud of the flanks in the hull's livery, the burner tank as an external cylinder near the engines, and the hold expanders as visibly larger cargo bays or an extra clamped container. Each is a small separate mesh attaching at the rig or thruster offsets in §05. The rest stay internal and need no geometry.

Mount markers P2

Empty mounts should be visible at close range: a capped socket, a blanking plate, an unarmed pylon. It is what tells a pilot looking at their own ship that there is somewhere left to put something.

World and interface (P1): dock_granted, dock_clamp, undock_launch, jump_engage, jump_arrive, loot_scoop, mining_impact, trade_confirm, trade_refused, skill_complete — a small satisfying chime, because this fires when something trained while you were away — and the four UI clicks.

Ambience and music P2

Five ambiences: high-security space (calm, distant traffic chatter, a low tonal bed), low-security (sparser, colder, irregular unexplained noises), asteroid field (pebble impacts on the hull, dust hiss), station interior (ventilation, machinery, muffled voices), and the bar (conversation, glasses, a bad jukebox).

Music is orchestral-electronic in Freelancer's register: melodic, unhurried, never busy. Loop cleanly and leave silence — this is a game people play for hours.

12Typography

The HUD is monospaced by design: the columns line up and the panels are tables. Bevy's bundled font has no em dash and no box-drawing characters — every on-screen string in this codebase is plain ASCII, and there are tests asserting it.

Needed: one monospaced family with a technical, slightly condensed feel, in Regular and Bold, covering Latin-1 plus arrows and block-drawing (─ │ ┌ ┐ └ ┘ █ ▓ ▒ ░ ← → ↑ ↓). Supplying those would let the panel frames be drawn in text and the ASCII-only tests be relaxed. A second, proportional family for headings and station signage would help, but is P2.

14Deliberately not here

Nothing should be generated for a system that cannot show it yet.

  • Mine-layer hardware. HardpointKind::Mine exists in the model and no hull declares one — the content loader now refuses a mount nothing can fill, so this stays empty until there is a module.

  • Player structures. Deferred to sovereignty.
  • Jump holes. Hidden gates exist in the model and are filtered out of what the client is told; nothing discovers them yet.
